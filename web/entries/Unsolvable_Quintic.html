The Abel-Ruffini theorem and the unsolvability of the quintic

Lawrence C. Paulson 📧

October 28, 2026

Abstract

This entry proves the Abel--Ruffini theorem — a polynomial equation solvable by radicals has a solvable Galois group — and applies it to a concrete quintic to conclude that the general quintic is not solvable by radicals. We follow the corresponding mathlib formalisation. The forward direction — solvable by radicals implies solvable Galois group — is assembled on the session New_Algebra: cyclotomic and Kummer extensions have solvable (indeed abelian) Galois groups, solvability is preserved by the tower correspondence, and the normal closure of a radical tower is again a radical tower obtained by splicing the Galois conjugates of the original tower. For the concrete witness we study the rational quintic $\Phi = x^5 - 4x + 2$ and show that the Galois group of its splitting field over $\mathbb{Q}$ is not solvable. We prove that $\Phi$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Eisenstein's criterion at 2) and, using the AFP entry Sturm_Sequences, show that it has exactly three real roots and one conjugate pair of non-real roots. Complex conjugation therefore acts on the five roots as a transposition, while irreducibility makes the Galois action transitive; a transitive subgroup of $S_5$ containing a transposition is the whole of $S_5$, which is not solvable. Combining the two halves yields the headline result that $\Phi$ is not solvable by radicals.
